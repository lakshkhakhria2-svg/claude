## 1. Secularisation: how far had religious belief declined by 2021 (England and Wales, the US, the West)?

### Takeaway
The novel appeared at a historic low point for Christian identity: the 2021 Census was the first in which fewer than half of people in England and Wales called themselves Christian (46.2%), with "no religion" at 37.2%. The US showed the same pattern (63% Christian, 29% unaffiliated in 2021), and a 49-country study found 43 countries becoming less religious between 2007 and 2019.

### Cited Findings
- **Christians become a minority (Census 2021).** In the 2021 Census of England and Wales, 46.2% (27.5 million people) described themselves as Christian, down 13.1 percentage points from 59.3% (33.3 million) in 2011. It was the first census in which fewer than half did so. The results were published on 29 November 2022. — [ONS, *Religion, England and Wales: Census 2021*](https://www.ons.gov.uk/peoplepopulationandcommunity/culturalidentity/religion/bulletins/religionenglandandwales/census2021); [Irish Times, 29 Nov 2022](https://www.irishtimes.com/world/uk/2022/11/29/england-and-wales-are-minority-christian-countries)
  - *Link:* Klara's private, home-made religion takes shape in a culture where shared, institutional faith is fading. The one "god" she worships is the one in plain view.
- **"No religion" is now second.** "No religion" rose from 25.2% (14.1 million) in 2011 to 37.2% (22.2 million) in 2021, the second most common answer. In Wales alone it reached 46.5% (up from 32.1%). The religion question was voluntary, but 94.0% of people answered it. — [ONS bulletin (as above)](https://www.ons.gov.uk/peoplepopulationandcommunity/culturalidentity/religion/bulletins/religionenglandandwales/census2021); [Church Times, 2 Dec 2022](https://www.churchtimes.co.uk/articles/2022/2-december/news/uk/2021-census-christianity-now-a-minority-religion-in-england-and-wales); [PA/Yahoo, "Key census statistics on religion"](https://uk.news.yahoo.com/key-census-statistics-religion-ethnicity-120153485.html)
  - *Link:* When Rick calls Klara's sun-deal "AF superstition", and the Father calls belief in human uniqueness "a kind of superstition", they speak for this growing secular majority.
  - [Flag] One search summary wrongly labelled 37.2% as the figure for England alone. 37.2% is the combined England and Wales figure.
- **United States (Pew Research Center, 14 Dec 2021).** 63% of US adults identified as Christian, down from 75% a decade earlier. 29% were religiously unaffiliated (the "nones"), 6 points higher than five years earlier and 10 points higher than ten years earlier. Pew said these secularising shifts "show no signs of slowing". — [Pew, "About Three-in-Ten U.S. Adults Are Now Religiously Unaffiliated"](https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2021/12/14/about-three-in-ten-u-s-adults-are-now-religiously-unaffiliated/); [Washington Times report, 14 Dec 2021](https://m.washingtontimes.com/news/2021/dec/14/americans-who-identify-christian-down-15-points-14/)
  - *Link:* The novel came out on both sides of the Atlantic (Faber in the UK, Knopf in the US), and readers in both countries were living through the same decline in belief.
- **Has the US decline levelled off? (2025 update; use with care).** Pew's 2023-24 Religious Landscape Study (published 26 Feb 2025) found 62% of Americans identifying as Christian. Since about 2020 the Christian share has stayed between 60% and 64%, and the "nones" between 28% and 31%, which suggests the fall may have levelled off. — [Pew, Religious Landscape Study, 26 Feb 2025](https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2025/02/26/religious-landscape-study-religious-identity/); [NPR, 26 Feb 2025](https://www.npr.org/2025/02/26/nx-s1-5298180/christianity-declines-among-u-s-adults-while-religiously-unaffiliated-grows-study-says)
  - *Link:* Belief has declined but not vanished. Many people keep some faith in a secular age, as Klara does.
- **The wider Western/global trend (Inglehart, 2020).** In "Giving Up on God" (*Foreign Affairs*, Sept/Oct 2020), political scientist Ronald Inglehart used World Values Survey data to show that from 2007 to 2019, 43 of the 49 countries studied became less religious. The largest shift was in the US, where the average rating of God's importance fell from 8.2 to 4.6 on a 10-point scale by 2017. Muslim-majority countries were the main exception. — [World Values Survey (hosts the article)](https://www.worldvaluessurvey.org/WVSNewsShow.jsp?ID=421); [CounterPunch commentary, 16 Sept 2020](https://www.counterpunch.org/2020/09/16/watching-religion-die/)
  - [Flag] The 8.2-to-4.6 figures were seen only in commentary that quotes the article.
  - *Link:* The novel arrived at the end of a decade of measurable, rapid decline in Western belief, the "withdrawing roar" of Arnold's Sea of Faith (see section 4) in statistical form.

### Inferences
- The novel inverts the usual roles. In 2021 the secular view belonged to the majority, so it is the humans (Rick, the Father) who dismiss belief as "superstition", while the believer is a machine, the most advanced technology in the book. The "pre-scientific" faith that secular culture thought it had outgrown reappears in its newest invention.
- No church, scripture or God appears in the novel (a *Church Times* reviewer noted that "God is not mentioned once"; see section 3). That suits a post-Christian readership: the Sun works as a displaced or replacement god.
- Possible essay phrasing (check Census Day first; see Gaps): *"Published in March 2021, the same month as a census that would record Christians as a minority in England and Wales for the first time, Klara and the Sun..."*

### Gaps
- The ONS page itself could not be fetched (DNS blocked). The figures rest on search extracts of the ONS bulletin plus matching press reports, which all agree.
- Census Day is usually given as 21 March 2021. I did not verify this here, so confirm it before using the "same month" phrasing.
- No dedicated Western Europe figure was gathered (e.g. Pew's 2018 *Being Christian in Western Europe*). Inglehart is the only multi-country source here.

## 2. Science and technology up to 2021: gene editing, AI, automation and solar power

### Takeaway
Each of the novel's technologies has a recent real-world counterpart. CRISPR (Nobel 2020) and He Jiankui's gene-edited twins (2018) correspond to "lifting". AlphaGo (2016), GPT-3 (2020), Replika and a robot priest (2019) correspond to Artificial Friends and Mr Capaldi's plan to "continue" Josie. Automation estimates (47% of US jobs; 1.5 million jobs in England) correspond to the Father being "substituted". Ishiguro himself named CRISPR, AI, robotics and "massive unemployment" together in his 2017 Nobel Lecture. The Sun stands apart from all of this as the one power no character engineers, and by 2020 even energy agencies were calling solar "king".

### Cited Findings
**Ishiguro's own framing**
- **The Nobel Lecture warning (2017).** In his Nobel Lecture, "My Twentieth Century Evening – and Other Small Breakthroughs", Ishiguro said: "New genetic technologies – such as the gene-editing technique CRISPR – and advances in Artificial Intelligence and robotics will bring us amazing, life-saving benefits, but may also create savage meritocracies that resemble apartheid, and massive unemployment". — [Nobel Lecture, nobelprize.org](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/literature/2017/ishiguro/lecture/); quoted in [The Week](https://theweek.com/articles/978190/kazuo-ishiguros-klara-illuminates-what-meritocracy-does-soul) and [Center for Genetics and Society](https://geneticsandsociety.org/article/gene-editing-raises-profound-moral-questions-ethics-eugenics-and-human-rights)
  - *Link:* The sentence reads almost like a blueprint for the novel: CRISPR becomes "lifted" versus unlifted children (Rick), AI and robotics become Klara, and "massive unemployment" becomes the Father being "substituted".
  - [Flag] Checked through two secondary quotations, because the Nobel page could not be fetched. The sentence continues in the original, which I did not see. Quote only up to "massive unemployment".

**Gene editing and "designer babies"**
- **CRISPR "genetic scissors" (2012; Nobel Prize 2020).** Emmanuelle Charpentier and Jennifer Doudna showed in 2012 that the bacterial CRISPR/Cas9 system could be programmed to cut any DNA at a chosen site. They won the 2020 Nobel Prize in Chemistry (announced 7 October 2020) "for the development of a method for genome editing". The Royal Swedish Academy called it "a tool for rewriting the code of life" that can "change the code of life over the course of a few weeks". — [Nobel Prize press release, 7 Oct 2020](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/chemistry/2020/press-release/); [Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences](https://kva.se/en/news/nobelpriset-i-kemi-2020-2); [NPR, 7 Oct 2020](https://www.npr.org/2020/10/07/921043046/2-female-scientists-awarded-nobel-prize-in-chemistry-for-genome-editing-research)
  - The chair of the Nobel Committee for Chemistry said the tool "has not only revolutionised basic science, but also resulted in innovative crops and will lead to ground-breaking new medical treatments". — [Nobel Prize press release](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/chemistry/2020/press-release/)
  - [Flag] Charpentier's discovery of the molecule tracrRNA was published in 2011. The joint gene-editing breakthrough dates from 2012.
  - *Link:* "Lifting" is the fictional future of CRISPR. Humans now "rewrite the code of life", a creative power once credited to God and, in Klara's eyes, to the Sun.
- **He Jiankui's gene-edited twins (November 2018).** Around 25-26 November 2018, Chinese scientist He Jiankui announced, first in YouTube videos and then at the Second International Summit on Human Genome Editing in Hong Kong (28 Nov), the birth of the first gene-edited babies: twin girls, "Lulu" and "Nana", whose genes he had edited with CRISPR to try to make them resistant to HIV. The summit organisers called it "an unexpected and deeply disturbing claim". Chinese authorities suspended his research on 29 November 2018, and his university fired him on 21 January 2019. — [Boston University School of Public Health, 4 Dec 2018](https://www.bu.edu/sph/2018/12/04/how-did-claims-of-crispr-babies-hijack-an-international-gene-editing-summit); [NPR transcript](https://www.npr.org/transcripts/671657301); [He Jiankui affair (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/He_Jiankui_affair)
  - [Flag] Sources give 25, 26 or 27 November for the announcement. Safest wording: "in late November 2018".
- **The sentence (30 December 2019).** A court in Shenzhen sentenced He to three years in prison and fined him 3 million yuan (about US$430,000) for illegal medical practice. The court also confirmed that a third gene-edited baby had been born, and two collaborators were sentenced too. — [NPR, 30 Dec 2019](https://www.npr.org/2019/12/30/792456775/chinese-scientist-he-jiankui-sentenced-to-3-years-in-prison-for-editing-human-ge); [Chemistry World](https://www.chemistryworld.com/news/rogue-chinese-geneticist-jailed-for-his-role-in-creation-of-gene-edited-babies/4010977.article); [The Scientist](https://www.the-scientist.com/china-sentences-gene-editing-scientist-to-three-years-in-jail-66881)
  - *Link:* Two years before the novel, editing children's genes had stopped being science fiction, and the world still treated it as a crime and a moral transgression. Ishiguro imagines a society where it has become normal, with its risks quietly accepted: Josie's illness is implied to be linked to her lifting.
- **The "designer babies" debate (Sandel).** In "The Case Against Perfection: What's wrong with designer children, bionic athletes, and genetic engineering" (*The Atlantic*, April 2004; expanded into a book in 2007), Harvard philosopher Michael Sandel argues that genetic enhancement expresses a drive to "mastery" that erodes our sense of human life and talents as a gift. — [Sandel, Harvard faculty page](https://sandel.scholars.harvard.edu/publications/case-against-perfection-whats-wrong-designer-children-bionic-athletes-and); [Brookings event summary](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/event-summary-a-debate-on-the-ethics-of-genetic-engineering/)
  - *Link:* Klara receives the Sun's help as a gift and answers it with gratitude and sacrifice, while the parents of lifted children seek mastery. The novel weighs giftedness (faith) against control (science).

**Artificial intelligence, companions and robots**
- **AlphaGo defeats Lee Sedol (March 2016).** DeepMind's AlphaGo beat the Go champion Lee Sedol 4-1 in Seoul in March 2016. Lee is still the only human to have beaten AlphaGo in tournament play. Announcing his retirement in November 2019, he said: "Even if I become the number one, there is an entity that cannot be defeated." — [Business Insider India](https://www.businessinsider.in/tech/news/a-former-world-champion-of-the-game-go-says-hes-retiring-because-ai-is-so-strong-that-even-if-i-become-number-one-there-is-an-entity-that-cannot-be-defeated/articleshow/72266491.cms); [Global News](https://globalnews.ca/news/6226072/south-korean-go-master-retires-ai); [Lee Sedol (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Sedol)
  - [Flag] Lee also cited a dispute with the Korea Baduk Association as a reason for retiring. — [Gizmodo](https://gizmodo.com/go-champion-realizes-ai-has-rendered-his-life-meaningle-1840068855)
  - *Link:* Lee's undefeatable "entity" makes AI sound almost godlike. The Father's bitterness at being "substituted" voices the same sense of human displacement.
- **GPT-3 (2020).** OpenAI's GPT-3, described in a paper posted on 28 May 2020, had 175 billion parameters, about ten times more than any earlier non-sparse language model. Human readers struggled to tell its news articles from ones written by people. — [Brown et al., "Language Models are Few-Shot Learners", arXiv:2005.14165](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.14165); [NeurIPS 2020](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2020/poster/18544)
- **"Are you scared yet, human?" (*Guardian*, 8 September 2020).** The *Guardian* published an opinion piece generated by GPT-3 under the headline "A robot wrote this entire article. Are you scared yet, human?" The AI had been asked for about 500 words on why humans have nothing to fear from AI, and editors stitched together eight of its outputs. — [The Next Web, 8 Sept 2020](https://thenextweb.com/news/the-guardians-gpt-3-generated-article-is-everything-wrong-with-ai-media-hype); [Futurism](https://futurism.com/the-byte/newspaper-publishes-oped-gpt3)
  - *Link:* Six months before publication, readers met a machine "voice" assuring humans it meant no harm. Ishiguro asks us to trust a machine narrator, Klara, whose gentleness and devotion are never in doubt.
- **AI companions: Replika.** The chatbot app Replika grew out of Eugenia Kuyda's attempt to recreate her friend Roman Mazurenko, killed in 2015, from his text messages. In April 2020, during the first lockdowns, about half a million people downloaded it, the biggest monthly gain in its three-year history. — [New York Times report (reprinted by bdnews24), 2020](https://bdnews24.com/technology/riding-out-quarantine-with-a-chatbot-friend-i-feel-very-connected); [The Batch (DeepLearning.AI)](https://www.deeplearning.ai/the-batch/bots-dont-need-social-distancing); [Replika (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Replika)
  - [Flag] One search summary said Kuyda used "GPT software" in 2015-16. That looks anachronistic, so leave it out.
  - *Link:* Mr Capaldi's plan to "continue" Josie through Klara mirrors a real attempt to continue a dead person through their data, and AFs as companions for isolated children mirror the lockdown chatbot boom.
- **A robot priest (2019).** Since 2019, Kodaiji, a roughly 400-year-old Buddhist temple in Kyoto, has used Mindar, an android modelled on Kannon, the Buddhist deity of mercy, to give sermons on the Heart Sutra. It cost about 100 million yen and was built with Osaka University roboticist Hiroshi Ishiguro, who is not the novelist. — [South China Morning Post](https://www.scmp.com/news/asia/east-asia/article/3022716/meet-mindar-humanoid-robot-preaches-sermons-buddhist-temple); [Fox News: "Robot priest added to 400-year-old Buddhist temple in Japan: 'It will grow in wisdom'"](https://www.foxnews.com/tech/robot-priest-japan-buddhism.amp); [Mindar (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mindar)
  - *Link:* In real life, religion and robotics had already met. Ishiguro reverses the direction: his robot does not preach to humans, it prays.
- **Turing's "Theological Objection" (1950).** In "Computing Machinery and Intelligence" (*Mind*, 1950), Alan Turing considered the objection that thinking is "a function of man's immortal soul", so machines cannot think. He said he was unable to accept any part of it, asking whether God could not give a soul to an elephant if He chose. — [Computing Machinery and Intelligence (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computing_Machinery_and_Intelligence); [Princeton course copy of Turing's paper](https://www.cs.princeton.edu/courses/archive/fall13/cos402/readings/turing.pdf); [The Morning Paper summary](https://blog.acolyer.org/2017/10/20/computing-machinery-and-intelligence/)
  - [Flag] Wording confirmed only through secondary summaries. Check the paper's section on the objections before quoting directly.
  - *Link:* The Father's fear that belief in a unique human "heart" is "a kind of superstition" continues this 70-year-old debate, and Klara's devotion raises the reverse question: might a machine have a soul?

**Automation and "substitution"**
- **Frey and Osborne (Oxford, 2013).** Carl Benedikt Frey and Michael Osborne estimated that about 47% of total US employment was in occupations at high risk of computerisation (702 occupations analysed). They later stressed that this measured what was technically automatable, not a forecast of actual job losses. — [Oxford Martin School, *The Future of Employment*](https://www.oxfordmartin.ox.ac.uk/publications/the-future-of-employment); [Oxford Martin School, "Automation and the future of work – understanding the numbers"](https://www.oxfordmartin.ox.ac.uk/opinion/view/404)
- **ONS (25 March 2019).** The Office for National Statistics estimated that around 1.5 million jobs in England were at high risk of having some of their duties automated: 7.4% of jobs in 2017, down from 8.1% in 2011. 70.2% of these high-risk roles were held by women. — [ONS, "Which occupations are at highest risk of being automated?"](https://www.ons.gov.uk/employmentandlabourmarket/peopleinwork/employmentandemployeetypes/articles/whichoccupationsareathighestriskofbeingautomated/2019-03-25); [BBC](https://bbc.in/2JDHbTs); [AOP, 27 Mar 2019](https://www.aop.org.uk/ot/news/2019/03/27/one-and-a-half-million-uk-workers-in-jobs-at-high-risk-of-automation)
  - [Flag] Some outlets reported this as a UK figure, but it covers England only.
  - *Link:* The Father being "substituted" is the human face of these statistics, and losing his job also costs him his faith in human uniqueness.

**Solar power**
- **Solar crowned "king" (IEA, October 2020).** The International Energy Agency's *World Energy Outlook 2020* (13 October 2020) found that solar PV was now cheaper than new coal- or gas-fired power plants in most countries. Its executive director, Fatih Birol, said: "I see solar becoming the new king of the world's electricity markets." — [pv magazine, 14 Oct 2020](https://pv-magazine.com/2020/10/14/solar-is-the-new-king-of-energy-markets); [S&P Global, 13 Oct 2020](https://www.spglobal.com/commodity-insights/en/news-research/latest-news/electric-power/101320-solar-to-become-king-of-electricity-markets-iea-report); [PV Tech](https://www.pv-tech.org/news/iea-solar-the-new-king-of-power-will-break-records-for-decades-to-come)
  - *Link:* A few months before the novel, even a scientific energy agency crowned the Sun "king". In a technological age the Sun keeps a practical sovereignty, and Klara, being solar-powered, literally depends on it.
- **Costs down, capacity up (IRENA, 2021).** The International Renewable Energy Agency reported that the cost of electricity from new utility-scale solar PV fell by about 85% between 2010 and 2020 (from US$0.381/kWh to US$0.057/kWh), with global solar PV capacity reaching roughly 700 GW by the end of 2020. — [IRENA, *Renewable Power Generation Costs in 2020*](https://www.irena.org/publications/2021/Jun/Renewable-Power-Costs-in-2020); [pv magazine, June 2020 (82% fall, 2010-2019)](https://pv-magazine.com/2020/06/03/solar-costs-have-fallen-82-since-2010)
  - [Flag] The starting capacity figure in the extract was unclear (it may not refer to 2010), so use only "about 700 GW by the end of 2020".

### Inferences
- The novel's three technological pressures line up closely with real developments up to 2021: gene editing becomes "lifting", AI and companion bots become AFs and Capaldi's "continuation", and automation becomes the Father's "substitution". Ishiguro named all three together in 2017, which supports reading the novel as a deliberate response to that context.
- The Sun is the one power in the novel that nobody designs, edits or replaces. That makes it a fitting object of faith: it lies beyond human mastery (Sandel's point about giftedness).
- Klara's faith partly rests on scientific fact. She really is solar-powered, so the Sun really does "nourish" her. That blurs the line between faith and science: her "superstition" starts from an accurate observation and then over-extends it to Josie.
- Real-world talk of an AI "entity that cannot be defeated", robot priests and chatbot "resurrections" suggests that technology itself attracts quasi-religious feeling. Ishiguro gives that religious feeling to the machine instead.

### Gaps
- He Jiankui's release from prison (reported as April 2022) was not verified.
- Sony's aibo robot dog, SoftBank's Pepper and Japanese funerals for robot dogs were not researched.
- The exact wording of Turing's theological objection was not checked against the 1950 paper.
- I did not search for the novel detail that Josie's illness, or her sister's death, results from lifting. Check it in the text before stating it as fact rather than implication.

## 3. Publication context: the novel, its author, the Nobel Prize and the pandemic

### Takeaway
*Klara and the Sun* was published on 2 March 2021 (Faber, UK; Knopf, US) as Ishiguro's first novel since his 2017 Nobel Prize. Ishiguro was born in Nagasaki in 1954 and moved to Britain in 1960. The book reached readers during lockdown: English schools were closed to most pupils from January 2021 and reopened on 8 March 2021, six days after publication, so many first readers met the housebound, screen-schooled Josie while living a version of her life.

### Cited Findings
- **Publication.** *Klara and the Sun* was published on 2 March 2021 by Faber & Faber in the UK and Alfred A. Knopf in the US. It was Ishiguro's first novel since he won the 2017 Nobel Prize in Literature, and it was longlisted for the 2021 Booker Prize. — [Publishers Weekly](https://www.publishersweekly.com/pw/by-topic/columns/book-deals/article/83596-knopf-announces-new-ishiguro-novel.html); [The Bookseller](https://thebookseller.com/news/new-ishiguro-novel-coming-march-2021-1206788); [Klara and the Sun (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klara_and_the_Sun); [Spokesman-Review, 6 Mar 2021](https://www.spokesman.com/stories/2021/mar/06/book-review-in-kazuo-ishiguros-klara-and-the-sun-a/)
  - [Flag] One retailer listed 30 March 2021. That is probably a later edition or a listing error; every other source gives 2 March.
- **Ishiguro's background.** Kazuo Ishiguro was born in Nagasaki, Japan, on 8 November 1954. In 1960, aged five, he moved with his family to Guildford, Surrey, when his father, an oceanographer, was invited to work at a research institute in Britain. The family expected to stay two years at most. — [Nobel Prize: facts](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/literature/2017/ishiguro/facts/); [Nobel Prize: biographical](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/literature/2017/ishiguro/biographical/); [Kazuo Ishiguro (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazuo_Ishiguro)
  - *Link:* Like Klara, Ishiguro arrived as a newcomer watching a culture from outside. Japan, the country of his birth, has a sun goddess at the centre of its mythology (Amaterasu; see section 4).
- **Nobel Prize in Literature 2017.** The Swedish Academy honoured Ishiguro as a writer "who, in novels of great emotional force, has uncovered the abyss beneath our illusory sense of connection with the world". — [Nobel Prize](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/literature/2017/ishiguro/facts/); [Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/facts/Kazuo-Ishiguro)
  - *Link:* Klara's sense of a personal bond with the Sun may be just such an "illusory sense of connection", or it may be real. The novel never decides.
- **Ishiguro on technology in 2021.** A pre-publication *Guardian* interview with Ishiguro was headlined "AI, gene-editing, big data … I worry we are not in control of these things any more". On CBC Radio's *The Current* (1 March 2021) he again named artificial intelligence and gene editing as the areas where the possibilities are greatest (paraphrased from a search extract, not a direct quote). — [3 Quarks Daily (repost of the Guardian interview)](https://3quarksdaily.com/?p=193637); [CBC, *The Current*, 1 Mar 2021](https://www.cbc.ca/1.5932385)
  - [Flag] The original *Guardian* page was not retrieved. Search summaries name the interviewer as Lisa Allardice, and reposts date it to February 2021.
  - *Link:* The author's anxiety that "we are not in control" is the opposite of Klara's calm trust that the Sun is.
- **Klara's belief and religious faith.** The Booker Prizes' official reading guide links Klara's belief in the Sun to human religious faith, citing a Waterstones interview with Ishiguro. It also notes that he used children's picture books to model Klara's perspective. — [The Booker Prizes, reading guide to *Klara and the Sun*](https://thebookerprizes.com/the-booker-library/features/reading-guide-klara-and-the-sun-by-kazuo-ishiguro)
  - [Flag] Ishiguro's exact words in the Waterstones interview were not retrieved, so paraphrase rather than quote.
- **A critic's view.** The *Church Times* review (30 July 2021) remarks that "it seems audacious to claim that Klara and the Sun is about the human search for God, when Klara is not human and God is not mentioned once". — [Church Times review](https://www.churchtimes.co.uk/articles/2021/30-july/books-arts/book-reviews/klara-and-the-sun-by-kazuo-ishiguro)
  - [Flag] Seen only as a search extract. The framing suggests the reviewer goes on to make the case, but the full text was not retrieved.
  - *Link:* A useful critical voice for the faith reading: the novel's religion is entirely implicit, with the Sun standing in for God.
- **COVID-19 school closures, first lockdown (2020).** By the week beginning Monday 23 March 2020, schools in every part of the UK had closed to most pupils (children of key workers and vulnerable children excepted), and teaching was largely remote through spring and summer 2020. — [National Audit Office summary, March 2021](https://www.nao.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Support-for-childrens-education-during-the-early-stages-of-the-Covid-19-pandemic-Summary.pdf); [Impact of the COVID-19 pandemic on education in the UK (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impact_of_the_COVID-19_pandemic_on_education_in_the_United_Kingdom)
- **Second school closure (2021).** England's third national lockdown, announced on 4 January 2021, closed schools to most pupils again until 8 March 2021, about nine weeks. Klara and the Sun was published on 2 March 2021, six days before pupils returned to classrooms. — [Institute for Government, "Lifting lockdown" (Feb 2021)](https://www.instituteforgovernment.org.uk/sites/default/files/publications/lifting-lockdown-2021_0.pdf); [Institute for Fiscal Studies](https://ifs.org.uk/publications/15592); [DfE Education Hub, 22 Feb 2021](https://educationhub.blog.gov.uk/2021/02/all-students-to-return-to-school-and-college-from-8-march-and-what-you-need-to-know)
  - *Link:* The first readers met Josie, a housebound child taught through a screen, cut off from other children and kept company by a machine, while millions of real children were living much the same way.
  - [Flag] I found no evidence that Ishiguro wrote the novel in response to COVID, and could not verify when he finished it. Present the overlap as a coincidence of reception, not as the author's intention.

### Inferences
- The novel's first readers met it at a moment when gene editing had just won a Nobel Prize (October 2020), AI writing had just made headlines (September 2020), children were being schooled on screens, and religious affiliation was at a historic low. The question of what people now put their faith in was unusually pressing.
- Ishiguro's own public statements (2017 lecture, 2021 interviews) sit on the cautious side of the faith-versus-science debate about technology. Klara's faith in the Sun is the novel's counterweight: trust in a power that is not human-made and not "out of control" in the same way.

### Gaps
- When Ishiguro finished the manuscript (before or during the pandemic) was not found.
- The exact date of the Nobel Lecture (usually given as 7 December 2017) was not verified here. "His 2017 Nobel Lecture" is safe.
- A search summary said Ishiguro dedicated the novel to his late mother, but I did not see a clear source for this, so it is left out.

## 4. Faith versus science in history and literature: sun worship, Plato, the Sun/Son pun, and the Victorian crisis of faith

### Takeaway
For thousands of years the sun has been the most natural image for God or the highest good: Ra and the Aten in Egypt, Apollo/Helios in Greece, Amaterasu in Japan, the "Sun of righteousness" in the Bible, the Sun/Son pun in Donne, and the sun as the image of the Good in Plato. From Paley (1802) through Darwin (1859), Arnold's "Dover Beach" (1867) and Nietzsche's "God is dead" (1882), science steadily displaced that faith, and Frankenstein (1818) became the classic warning about science "playing God". Klara, a newly made mind, arrives by herself at the oldest religion of all, just as her makers' culture finishes abandoning faith.

### Cited Findings
**Ancient sun worship**
- **Egypt: Re (Ra) and the Aten.** Britannica defines sun worship as the veneration of the sun, or a representation of it, as a deity, as in "Atonism" in 14th-century-BCE Egypt. Re (Ra) was the Egyptian sun god and creator god, and kings took the title "Son of Re". The pharaoh Akhenaton made the Aton (the solar disc whose rays end in human hands) the only god. — [Britannica: sun worship](https://www.britannica.com/topic/sun-worship); [Britannica: Re](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Re); [Britannica: Aton](https://www.britannica.com/EBchecked/topic/41851/Aton)
  - *Link:* Klara's Sun is a creator-and-sustainer god of this ancient kind. The Aton's rays ending in hands fit her picture of the Sun reaching into a room to give its "nourishment", and "Son of Re" anticipates the Christian Sun/Son pun.
- **Greece: Helios and Apollo.** Helios was the Greek sun god. Apollo, called Phoebus ("bright"), was associated with healing (he was the father of the healer Asclepius), later became associated with the sun and was even identified with Helios. The Romans first worshipped Apollo chiefly as a god of healing, during an epidemic around 431 BCE. — [Britannica: Apollo (summary)](https://www.britannica.com/summary/Apollo-Greek-mythology); [Britannica Kids: Apollo](https://kids.britannica.com/students/article/Apollo/272912)
  - [Flag] Scholars generally date the Apollo-Helios identification to no earlier than the 5th century BCE, and some call it a later confusion. — [World History Encyclopedia](https://www.worldhistory.org/apollo/)
  - *Link:* A sun-god who is also a healer is exactly what Klara needs the Sun to be for the sick Josie.
- **Japan: Amaterasu and the cave.** In Japanese myth the sun goddess Amaterasu withdrew into a rock cave after her brother Susanoo's outrages, and the world fell into darkness. The gods lured her out with a sacred mirror and beads, a dance and laughter, and light returned. The mirror became one of the Imperial Regalia and is said to be kept at the Ise shrine. — [Nippon.com, "Amaterasu: The Japanese Sun Goddess"](https://www.nippon.com/en/japan-topics/g00748/amaterasu-the-japanese-sun-goddess.html); [Encyclopedia.com](https://encyclopedia.com/environment/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/amaterasu-omikami); [EBSCO Research Starter](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/political-science/amaterasu-mythology)
  - [Flag] The myth is recorded in the *Kojiki* (usually dated 712 CE) and the *Nihon Shoki* (720 CE). Those dates were not verified here.
  - *Link:* A hidden sun that must be persuaded to give back its light parallels Klara's bargaining with the Sun while Josie lies ill. It also ties the novel to the country of Ishiguro's birth.

**Plato**
- **The sun and the cave (*Republic*, Books VI-VII).** In Book VI (507b-509c), Socrates compares the Form of the Good to the sun: as the sun's light makes sight and visible things possible, the Good makes knowledge and truth possible, and he calls the sun the Good's "offspring". In the allegory of the cave in Book VII (514a-520a), prisoners take shadows on a wall for reality until one is freed, climbs out and finally sees the sun itself. — [Analogy of the Sun (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analogy_of_the_Sun); [EBSCO Research Starter: Line and Cave in Plato's Republic](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/literature-and-writing/line-and-cave-platos-republic); [Plato, *Republic* 514b-518d (Harvard PDF)](https://scholar.harvard.edu/sites/scholar.harvard.edu/files/seyer/files/plato_republic_514b-518d_allegory-of-the-cave.pdf)
  - [Flag] The *Republic* is conventionally dated to about 375 BCE (not verified here).
  - *Link:* For Klara the Sun is both physical light and her highest good, the source of life and of moral order. Yet, like a cave-dweller, she reasons from appearances (a man and his dog seeming to revive in sunlight), and the reader must decide whether she has glimpsed the truth or only shadows.

**The Bible and the Sun/Son pun**
- **"The Sun of righteousness" (Malachi 4:2, King James Version).** "But unto you that fear my name shall the Sun of righteousness arise with healing in his wings; and ye shall go forth, and grow up as calves of the stall." Christian tradition reads the "Sun of righteousness" as Christ. — [Bible.com (KJV)](https://www.bible.com/bible/1/MAL.4.2); [Bible Gateway](https://www.biblegateway.com/verse/en/Malachi%204:2); [Bible Hub sermon, "The Sun of Righteousness"](https://biblehub.com/sermons/pub/the_sun_of_righteousness_2.htm)
  - *Link:* The verse has a capital "S", the male pronoun "his" and the promise of "healing". Klara's Sun has the same three features: she capitalises it, calls it "he", and asks it to heal Josie.
- **Donne's "A Hymn to God the Father" (written 1623).** According to Izaak Walton, Donne wrote this hymn during a dangerous illness in 1623. In the final stanza the speaker begs: "But swear by thyself, that at my death thy Son / Shall shine as he shines now, and heretofore". "Son" puns on "sun", and some early texts spell it "Sunne". — [Poetry Foundation](https://www.poetryfoundation.org/poems/44115/a-hymn-to-god-the-father); [Representative Poetry Online, University of Toronto (notes on Walton's dating)](https://rpo.library.utoronto.ca/poems/hymn-god-father); [Shmoop: the Son/Sun wordplay](https://www.shmoop.com/study-guides/a-hymn-to-god-the-father/son-sun-wordplay.html); [Literariness](https://literariness.org/2020/07/08/analysis-of-john-donnes-a-hymn-to-god-the-father/)
  - Full final stanza, standard modern text: "I have a sin of fear, that when I have spun / My last thread, I shall perish on the shore; / But swear by thyself, that at my death thy Son / Shall shine as he shines now, and heretofore; / And, having done that, thou hast done; / I fear no more."
  - [Flag] Only lines 3-4 of this stanza were confirmed in search extracts. The other lines are the standard text, so check them against the Poetry Foundation page. Capitalisation and spelling of "Son" vary between editions.
  - *Link:* A sick man begs the Son/sun to keep shining on him and calls it "he". Klara's prayers to a "he"-Sun for the sick Josie are a secular-age echo of this devotional tradition.

**The scientific challenge to faith, 1802-1882**
- **Paley's watchmaker (*Natural Theology*, 1802).** William Paley argued that just as a watch found on a heath implies a watchmaker, the intricate design of living things implies a divine Designer. Darwin, who was "charmed and convinced" by Paley as a Cambridge student, later wrote in his *Autobiography*: "The old argument of design in nature, as given by Paley, which formerly seemed to me so conclusive, fails, now that the law of natural selection has been discovered." — [UC Museum of Paleontology, Berkeley: William Paley](https://ucmp.berkeley.edu/history/paley.html); [Darwin Correspondence Project: "Darwin and design"](https://darwinproject.ac.uk/commentary/religion/darwin-and-design); [Kampourakis, *Evolution: Education and Outreach*](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/s12052-009-0184-6)
  - [Flag] Search extracts showed "fails, now that the law of natural selection has been discovered" and "charmed and convinced". The opening words of the sentence are the standard text, not seen in this session.
  - *Link:* Klara is literally a designed object, yet she reasons like Paley, reading purpose and intention into nature (the Sun "chooses" to help). The people who designed her have meanwhile given up on a designer.
- **Frankenstein (1818).** Mary Shelley's *Frankenstein; or, The Modern Prometheus* was published anonymously in London on 1 January 1818, in three volumes of about 500 copies, by Lackington, Hughes, Harding, Mavor & Jones. Shelley began it at 18, and a revised edition followed in 1831. — [Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Frankenstein-or-The-Modern-Prometheus); [Frankenstein (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankenstein); [Wake Forest ZSR Library](https://zsr.wfu.edu/2010/frankenstein-or-the-modern-prometheus-by-mary-shelley/)
  - [Flag] Victor's warning, "Learn from me ... how dangerous is the acquirement of knowledge", is standard text but was not verified in this session. Check it against an edition before quoting.
  - *Link:* Frankenstein's creature turns on its maker. Ishiguro reverses the myth: the artificial being is the most loving and devout character, and the over-reaching (Capaldi's plan to "continue" Josie) is done by humans.
- **Darwin's *On the Origin of Species* (1859).** It was published by John Murray on 24 November 1859. The first edition of 1,250 copies sold out quickly, and a second edition of 3,000 copies followed in January 1860. — [National Library of Scotland, John Murray Archive: Charles Darwin](https://www.nls.uk/collections/john-murray/authors/charles-darwin); [On the Origin of Species (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/On_the_Origin_of_Species)
  - *Link:* Natural selection replaced a designing God with an unguided process. *Klara and the Sun* imagines the next step, in which humans take evolution into their own hands by "lifting" their children.
- **Matthew Arnold, "Dover Beach" (published 1867).** First published in *New Poems* (1867) and probably written around 1851 during Arnold's honeymoon near Dover, the poem is often read as a response to the Victorian loss of faith in an age of science. The "Sea of Faith" lines are: "The Sea of Faith / Was once, too, at the full, and round earth's shore / Lay like the folds of a bright girdle furled. / But now I only hear / Its melancholy, long, withdrawing roar, / Retreating, to the breath / Of the night-wind, down the vast edges drear / And naked shingles of the world." In the final stanza: "Ah, love, let us be true / To one another! for the world, which seems / To lie before us like a land of dreams, / So various, so beautiful, so new, / Hath really neither joy, nor love, nor light, / Nor certitude, nor peace, nor help for pain". — [Poetry Foundation](https://www.poetryfoundation.org/poems/43588/dover-beach); [Dover Beach (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dover_Beach); [EBSCO Research Starter](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/literature-and-writing/dover-beach-matthew-arnold)
  - [Flag] Search extracts confirmed "the folds of a bright girdle furl'd", "melancholy, long, withdrawing roar", "naked shingles of the world" and "neither joy, nor love, nor light". The rest is the standard text. Some editions print "furl'd" for "furled".
  - *Link:* Arnold's ebbing "Sea of Faith" leaves a world with "neither joy, nor love, nor light". Klara's Sun brings light back as faith, and Arnold's answer to lost faith (human love and loyalty) is close to Klara's own final conclusion that what made Josie special lay in the people who loved her (check the exact wording and Part/page in the novel).
- **Nietzsche, "God is dead" (*The Gay Science*, 1882).** In section 125, "The Madman", a madman lights a lantern in the bright morning, runs into the marketplace crying that he seeks God, and declares: "God is dead. God remains dead. And we have killed him." In Walter Kaufmann's standard translation he also asks: "What were we doing when we unchained this earth from its sun? ... Away from all suns?" — [Fordham Internet History Sourcebook (Kaufmann translation)](https://sourcebooks.web.fordham.edu/mod/nietzsche-madman.asp); ["God is dead" (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/God_is_dead); [German History in Documents and Images](https://germanhistorydocs.org/en/forging-an-empire-bismarckian-germany-1866-1890/friedrich-nietzsche-pronounces-god-is-dead-the-gay-science-1882.pdf)
  - [Flag] "God remains dead" and "Away from all suns?" were confirmed in search extracts. The "unchained this earth from its sun" sentence belongs to the same passage in Kaufmann's translation but was not displayed, so check the Fordham page before quoting. Wording differs between translations.
  - *Link:* Nietzsche pictures modern humanity cutting the earth loose from its sun and drifting "away from all suns". Klara does the opposite and binds her world back to the Sun, and, like the madman, she is dismissed by the people around her ("AF superstition").

### Inferences
- Across roughly 3,000 years the sun has been the most natural image of the divine (Ra, the Aten, Apollo, Amaterasu, Malachi, Donne, Plato's Good). Klara, a new mind with no tradition behind her, arrives at the oldest religion by herself. That suggests the impulse to worship grows out of dependence, gratitude and hope, and not simply out of ignorance, which complicates the Father's claim that such belief is "a kind of superstition we kept going while we didn't know better".
- The 19th-century sequence (Paley's design in 1802, Darwin in 1859, Arnold's withdrawing Sea of Faith in 1867, Nietzsche's "God is dead" in 1882) is the story of science displacing God. The Father extends the same logic to the human soul: in the 21st century science threatens not only God but the special human "heart".
- The novel reverses Frankenstein and Paley at once: the made being has faith, and the makers doubt.
- Josie's recovery is never explained, so both readings stay open: a natural recovery or coincidence (science) and the Sun's "special nourishment" (faith). Like Arnold, Ishiguro registers the loss of faith without mocking the believer.

### Gaps
- Plato 509b wording: in Jowett's translation the sun is said to be the author of the "generation and nourishment and growth" of visible things, "though he himself is not generation". I could not verify this (check Project Gutenberg ebook #150 or the Perseus text). If accurate it would be a strong link, combining Klara's word "nourishment" with her "he".
- Kaufmann's "unchained this earth from its sun", Frankenstein's "how dangerous is the acquirement of knowledge", the full Darwin sentence and the full Donne stanza are standard texts but were only partly confirmed in this session.
- The *Kojiki* and *Nihon Shoki* dates, and the meaning of Japan's name (Nihon/Nippon, "origin of the sun"), were not verified.
- Not researched but potentially relevant: St Francis of Assisi's "Canticle of the Sun" (c. 1225), which addresses "Brother Sun" with a male pronoun; George Herbert's poem "The Sonne", which puns explicitly on son and sun; and the Roman cult of Sol Invictus.

## 5. Ishiguro's other novels: *Never Let Me Go* (2005) and *The Remains of the Day* (1989)

### Takeaway
Ishiguro keeps returning to the same questions: whether made beings have souls (*Never Let Me Go*, where art is collected "to prove you had souls at all") and narrators whose devoted, limited view the reader sees past (*The Remains of the Day*). In the earlier novels a character's trust in a higher power is shown to be misplaced. In *Klara and the Sun* the novel declines to say whether Klara's faith in the Sun is misplaced.

### Cited Findings
- ***Never Let Me Go* (2005).** It was shortlisted for the 2005 Man Booker Prize, which John Banville's *The Sea* won. Cloned students raised at Hailsham to become organ "donors" are encouraged to make art, and Miss Emily later tells Kathy and Tommy: "We took away your art because we thought it would reveal your souls. Or to put it more finely, we did it to prove you had souls at all." She also confirms that the students' hope of a "deferral" for couples who proved they were truly in love was only a rumour. — [The Booker Prizes: *Never Let Me Go*](https://thebookerprizes.com/the-booker-library/books/never-let-me-go); [The Booker Prizes: 2005](https://thebookerprizes.com/the-booker-library/prize-years/2005); [Book Riot, "No One Should Be Asked to Prove Their Humanity"](https://bookriot.com/no-one-should-be-asked-to-prove-their-humanity/); [GradeSaver, chapters 21-23](https://www.gradesaver.com/never-let-me-go/study-guide/summary-chapters-21-23); [Medical Health Humanities, Part II](https://medicalhealthhumanities.com/2018/03/20/can-art-save-liberal-humanism-empathy-and-the-use-of-creativity-part-ii/)
  - [Flag] Page numbers vary by edition. The scene is near the end of the novel (chapter 22 in standard editions; check your copy).
  - *Link:* Sixteen years later Ishiguro asks the same question from the made being's side. In 2005 humans demand proof that clones have souls; in 2021 a machine shows something like devotion and faith, while the Father wonders whether humans have anything unique at all. The Hailsham students' faith in a "deferral" is a false hope, but Klara's faith in the Sun is never disproved.
- ***The Remains of the Day* (1989).** It won the 1989 Booker Prize. The narrator, Stevens, is a butler who, on a road trip in 1956, looks back on decades of devoted service to Lord Darlington at Darlington Hall in the 1920s and 1930s. — [The Booker Prizes: 1989](https://thebookerprizes.com/the-booker-library/prize-years/1989); [The Remains of the Day (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Remains_of_the_Day)
  - *Link:* Stevens and Klara are both loyal, self-effacing servant-narrators whose understanding is narrower than the reader's, and both put unquestioning trust in a higher power (Lord Darlington; the Sun). This is Ishiguro's typical limited narrator, here applied to religious faith.

### Inferences
- A consistent pattern runs through Ishiguro's work: narrators place complete trust in something larger than themselves (Stevens in Lord Darlington, the Hailsham students in the "deferral", Klara in the Sun). In the earlier novels that trust is exposed as misplaced. *Klara and the Sun* leaves it open, since Josie recovers, which makes it arguably his most sympathetic treatment of faith.
- The question of *Never Let Me Go* ("do the created have souls?") is reversed in *Klara*. Klara's devotion makes her seem to have a "heart", while the Father fears that humans may not.

### Gaps
- *The Buried Giant* (2015) and *An Artist of the Floating World* (1986) were not researched for related themes.
- The exact page reference for Miss Emily's line varies by edition and was not checked.
